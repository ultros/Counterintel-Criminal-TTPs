# Terminology methodology and evidentiary safeguards

## Purpose and limits

This atlas supplies vocabulary for analyzing conduct, not a tool for diagnosing individuals, accusing identifiable people, or determining legal culpability. It covers concepts drawn from psychology, criminology, forensic science, cybercrime and intelligence analysis, alongside clearly marked descriptive terms.

## Editorial classes

- **E — Recognized concept:** A term used in a relevant discipline or professional practice. That does not imply universal scientific consensus; exact disciplinary use and source verification are needed.
- **D — Descriptive working term:** An editorial analytical label, not a formally established construct unless independently documented.
- **Q — Qualified or contested:** A label with disputed applicability, boundaries, or common misuse.

Counts in this preview: **350 E, 140 D, and 10 Q**. Labels are provisional and under source review.

## Evidence ladder

1. **Observed fact:** What an original document, witness, recording, or system record actually supports.
2. **Corroborated evidence:** Independent material supporting an event or causal link.
3. **Reasonable inference:** A conclusion consistent with evidence, explicitly distinguished from direct observation.
4. **Testable hypothesis:** An explanation that can be compared with competing possibilities.
5. **Speculation / unknown:** Open questions without adequate supporting evidence.

## Applying a term responsibly

Document what occurred, when it occurred, which actors knew what, and what would have happened if the alleged influencing action had not occurred. Evaluate sources for independence and provenance. Consider benign mistakes, misunderstood communication, coincidence, and alternative causation. Specify what observable result would **disprove** the proposed explanation.

Matching a TTP label does not establish deliberate manipulation, an organized conspiracy, criminal intent, or legal liability.

## Publication and corrections

Each term should eventually include authoritative source references, disagreements in nomenclature, operational distinctions, counterexamples, and explicit status as a scientific term or working descriptor. Stable CP identifiers should not be renumbered silently. A proposed correction needs the CP ID, current wording, revised wording, and high-quality support.

## Editorial safeguards

Keep unverified allegations, identifying personal details, privileged material and unpublished case investigations outside the public terminology index. This project is not a substitute for legal, clinical or law-enforcement assessment.
